# The Persean Banking Confederation

> *"Fleets burn. Worlds fall. The Ledger remembers."*
> — motto above the Bullion Clearing Hall

The Persean Banking Confederation (PBC) is the oldest financial institution in the Sector, and some would argue the reason the credit is still worth anything at all.

---

## Origins: the night the money stopped

When the Gate network went dark, the Persean Sector lost more than ships, colonists and contact with the Core Worlds. It lost its **settlement layer**. Every credit in the Sector was a claim on Domain accounts that were reconciled, cycle after cycle, by clearing houses on the far side of the Gates. Overnight, no transfer could be confirmed, no debt verified, no balance proven. Payrolls failed. Fuel stations refused credit chits. Within months, entire colonies had fallen back to barter and ration cards.

One institution still held a complete copy of the books. The **Persean Fiscal Office**, a minor Domain treasury annex in a quiet yellow-star system later renamed *Aurum*, had kept a local mirror of every account in the Sector, as required by Domain audit law. Its staff were clerks, auditors and actuaries. They were not soldiers, and they had no orders. They did have the only ledger in the Sector that everyone could agree was true.

They made a deal with the three largest merchant houses that had survived the Collapse:

- **House Varenne**, hull and cargo insurers, who owned most of the Sector's surviving freight contracts;
- **the Okonkwo-Lisle Clearing House**, which ran inter-colony payments for the old mining concerns;
- **Tessaly Mercantile**, fuel brokers with depots in a dozen systems.

The merchants brought ships, guns and customers. The Fiscal Office brought the Ledger. The agreement they signed on Bullion, in the third cycle after the Collapse, is still the founding document of the Confederation: the **Bullion Charter**.

## The Bullion Charter

The Charter is short, and every Confederation officer can recite it. Its four articles are the reason the PBC has survived two centuries of war:

1. **The Ledger is one.** There is a single record of every account, and the Confederation keeps it. No faction, house or director may keep a second book.
2. **Every debt is a promise; every promise is recorded.** Debts do not vanish when a debtor dies, flees or wins a war. They are settled, written down, or discharged by law. They are never forgotten.
3. **The Confederation takes no side.** It lends to all who can repay, and refuses those who cannot, whatever their banner.
4. **The Confederation defends its books, not its borders.** Its fleets protect assets, collect debts and keep the clearing lanes open. They do not conquer.

Article 3 has been tested by every war the Sector has seen. The Confederation has funded both sides of most of them.

## Aurum: the vault of the Sector

The Confederation governs a single system, and has never sought another.

**Bullion** is a temperate terran world and the administrative heart of the Confederation. Its arcologies house the Clearing Hall, the Directorate, the actuarial colleges, and hundreds of thousands of clerks, analysts and their families. Bullion has no great industry. Its export is *trust*, and it is the richest world per capita in the Sector.

**Vault** is a barren, airless rock that holds the Ledger itself. The original Fiscal Office data cores sit kilometres below the surface, surrounded by redundant archives, physical reserves of rare metals, and automated defences that answer to no single officer. Three keys are needed to open the deep archive: one held by the High Director, one by the Chief Auditor, and one by the Fleet Director. Officially, nobody has used all three since the Charter was signed.

**Ledger** is a gas giant whose orbital station, **Ledger Station**, refines the fuel and runs the light industry that keeps the Confederation independent of its own clients. The founders insisted on it: *a bank that cannot fuel its own fleet is a bank that can be starved.*

The **Aurum Relay**, **Nav Buoy** and **Sensor Array** form the most closely watched approach in the Sector. Every ship that enters Aurum is logged. That data is kept as well.

## How the Confederation works

The Confederation is led by a **High Director**, elected for life by the **Board of Houses**. The Board seats the heirs of the three founding houses alongside the senior officers of the old Fiscal Office. Below the Board:

- **The Clearing Hall** settles trade between factions that will not speak to each other. A Hegemony freighter can sell to a Tri-Tachyon buyer because the payment passes through Bullion.
- **The Directorate of Branches** runs the Confederation's branch offices across the Sector. Each is led by a **Branch Director**. Branch offices are on neutral ground by treaty, and most governments honour that even in wartime.
- **The Office of Standing** keeps the credit file of every captain, colony and government the Confederation deals with (see below).
- **The Sovereign Desk** underwrites government bonds and trades them.
- **The Recovery Directorate** commands the Collection Fleets, under a **Fleet Director**, with a **Security Chief** responsible for Aurum's own patrols.

PBC officers are famously dull. They wear charcoal and gold, speak in figures, and never raise their voices. Spacers say that if a PBC officer starts sounding friendly, you should check your balance.

## The Office of Standing

The Confederation does not lend on reputation, family or flag. It lends on **Standing**: a number between 300 and 850, computed once a month from the record of everything a borrower has promised and delivered.

The scale is older than the Confederation. The founding actuaries inherited it from Domain credit law, which inherited it from something older still. Nobody remembers why it starts at 300. Nobody has proposed changing it.

The rules of Standing are public, and a borrower can read them in any branch office:

- **Payment history matters most.** Every month a loan is honoured is recorded. Every month it is not is recorded too.
- **Lenders report lateness only after thirty days.** A missed day is a mistake. A missed month is a mark.
- **Marks fade, but slowly.** A late payment weighs heavily for a cycle, less after two, and leaves the file after seven. A charge-off or repossession stays the full seven cycles. A **discharge in bankruptcy** stays ten.
- **Every application is an inquiry.** Captains who borrow and repay in the same week to look reliable learn quickly that the Office of Standing counts the asking, not just the paying. Applications for the same kind of credit within forty-five days are counted as one, because shopping for a good rate is prudence, not desperation.
- **No history, no Standing.** A new name has no number until an account with the Confederation has run for six months. Until then, it gets starter credit and nothing more.

The Office of Standing does not care who you are, what you fly, or whom you have killed. Pirate lords with clean files have borrowed at better rates than Hegemony admirals. The Office considers this a feature.

## The Collection Fleets

Article 4 of the Charter lets the Confederation defend its books, and the Recovery Directorate interprets "defend" broadly.

When a debt falls badly past due, the Confederation follows a fixed procedure, unchanged for two centuries:

1. **A reminder.** Courteous, precise and itemised.
2. **A restriction.** The debtor's branch access is frozen, and penalty interest begins.
3. **A Collection Fleet.** A warfleet under a Recovery Directorate captain is dispatched to find the debtor. The captain will hail before firing, state the account number and the amount, and offer terms: pay in full, settle, surrender a ship as collateral, or ask for time. Refusing is permitted. It is just very expensive.
4. **Default.** The debt is charged off, the debtor's holdings at the Confederation are seized, colony income is garnished, and the mark goes on the file.

Debts secured by a colony follow a different road. A defaulted colony is first placed in **receivership**: Confederation administrators take over its books and collect its income until the debt is current. If that fails, the Recovery Directorate forecloses and takes the colony. It never keeps it. Article 4 forbids conquest, so every foreclosed world goes to auction on Bullion, where the great powers bid for it. The proceeds settle the debt, and anything left over is returned to the debtor, to the credit.

Destroying a Collection Fleet does not clear a debt. The Recovery Directorate records the loss, adds it to the cost of collection, and sends a larger fleet. Spacers say the PBC has never lost a war, because it has never fought one: it has only ever collected.

## The Sovereign Desk

Governments borrow too. The Hegemony funds its patrols, the Diktat its fuel subsidies, and the League its shipyards with Confederation bonds. The Sovereign Desk prices them on stability, territory and war. A faction at war pays more. A faction that defaults sees its bonds collapse, its credit frozen, and every branch office in its space quietly packing its archives.

Sovereign defaults are rare, and they reshape the Sector when they happen. The Desk keeps the bond tables public, and some say the market moves before the fleets do.

## The Confederation and the Sector

| Faction | Standing with the PBC | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Persean League** | Cordial | The League's member worlds are the Confederation's best customers, and League bonds are the most traded paper in the Sector. Kazeron sends an observer to the Board of Houses, without a vote. |
| **Hegemony** | Correct | The Hegemony tolerates the Confederation because its own payroll clears through Bullion. It also distrusts the data cores at Vault. Under the **Chicomoztoc Accord**, Hegemony AI inspectors may audit the deep archive's outer levels once per cycle. They have never found anything. They have never been shown everything. |
| **Tri-Tachyon** | Polite rivalry | Tri-Tachyon has offered to buy the Ledger at least four times, and once tried to copy it. The Confederation still lends to Tri-Tachyon, at a premium. |
| **Sindrian Diktat** | Cautious | The Diktat borrows heavily against future fuel sales. The Sovereign Desk watches its debt the way the Diktat watches its citizens. |
| **Independents** | Business as usual | Most free colonies hold their reserves with the Confederation because no one else will hold them. |
| **Luddic Church** | Wary | The Church preaches against usury but moves its tithes through Bullion, and has never asked the Confederation to stop. The Confederation has never mentioned it. |
| **Luddic Path** | Hostile | To the Path, the Ledger is an idol and Vault is its temple. There have been at least three attempts to bomb the deep archive. None reached the second level. |
| **Pirates** | Hostile | Pirates are the Recovery Directorate's natural enemy, and its most reliable source of salvage. Persistent rumours speak of a **"grey ledger"** where some pirate captains keep accounts under false names. The Confederation denies it, in writing. |
| **Remnant** | Hostile | Automated fleets cannot sign contracts. The Confederation considers this disqualifying. |

## Notable figures

- **High Director Ilse Varenne-Okafor**, current head of the Confederation and a descendant of two founding houses. She is known for answering every question with a figure and every threat with a rate increase.
- **Chief Auditor Tomas Reyl**, keeper of the second key to the deep archive. He has not left Vault in eleven cycles and is said to know every account in the Sector by number.
- **Fleet Director Amara Sosa-Lind**, head of the Recovery Directorate. She has never personally lost a collection, and keeps the transponder of every Collection Fleet destroyed in the line of duty on the wall of her office.

## Rumours and hooks

- **The fourth key.** Some insist the deep archive was built for four keys, not three, and that the fourth was never found.
- **The Domain balance.** If the Gates ever reopen, the Ledger would show what the Sector owes the Core Worlds, and what the Core Worlds owe the Sector. Some on the Board believe the Confederation would then be the largest creditor in human space.
- **Standing for sale.** Every few cycles, someone claims to sell a clean file. The Office of Standing has always found them, and always written it down.
- **The grey ledger.** If it exists, somebody at the Confederation keeps it. If it does not, why do pirate raiders never touch a PBC freighter?
