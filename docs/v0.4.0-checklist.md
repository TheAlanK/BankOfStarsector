# v0.4.0-beta — "The Sector's Economy"

Three systems: the PBC rewards a good relationship, every investment return has a real source in the Sector, and factions finance each other's development.

Branch: `release/v0.4.0-beta` (from `main` at v0.3.2-beta). Effort: S = a day or two, M = a few days, L = a week, XL = more.

**Decisions (user, 2026-10-11):**
- Investment returns come from **real money flows**.
- NPC-to-NPC deals **really build the industry** in the NPC market.
- The PBC relationship raises an **internal underwriting score**; the bureau score stays FICO-pure.
- 0.3.2 (realistic returns) was released first.

**Order:** 0 → A → B → C → R. A is small. B is the foundation C needs, because faction treasuries decide who can invest and who can repay.

Rules carried over from v0.3.0:
- Save compatibility guard: only additive, null-safe saved fields.
- NexusUI updated for every visible change.
- No per-frame sector scans: monthly or cached per game day only.
- Strings in en and pt-BR.
- Each section ends with `test.ps1 -Smoke` passing.

## A. Relationship banking (S) ✅
The PBC rewards customers who pay on time and penalizes those who don't. The bonuses are small and grow with the relationship.

**Design**
- [x] Relationship `r` is the PBC's relation to the player, from −1 to +1.
- [x] **Internal score.** The PBC's underwriting score is the bureau score + round(25 × r), clamped to 300–850.
  - It drives eligibility, loan limits, the number of loans, the credit-line limit and the rate bracket.
  - No bureau score (a thin file) means no internal score.
  - The bureau score never includes it. The credit-builder loan's eligibility and the insurance credit factor keep using the bureau score: the builder loan is for thin or damaged files, and insurers price on bureau data.
- [x] **Relationship pricing.** Loan rates × (1 − 0.10 × r).
- [x] **Investment returns.** Expected annual return + 0.5% × r.
- [x] **Behaviour.**
  - +0.002 per bill paid on time (from the second bill on; credit lines only while in use).
  - +0.01 per loan paid off after 3+ months. Taking and repaying at once earns nothing.
  - −0.02 per missed payment.
  - −0.08 at default.
  - Banking alone moves the relation between *Inhospitable* and *Friendly*. Hostility only comes from refusing a Collection Fleet or a foreclosure war. A defaulted borrower in receivership for years ends at −0.49, never hostile.
- [x] The monthly statement shows the relation change since the last month end ("PBC relations +0.4").

**Implementation**
- [x] `CreditScoreManager`: `pbcRelationship()`, `relationshipBonus()`, `getUnderwritingScore()`, `adjustRelation()` (with the cap and floor `RepLevel`s), and `relationChangeThisMonth()`. Two new saved fields, null-safe.
- [x] Underwriting call sites (loan offers, quotes, the branch, credit line, max loans, rate modifier) use the internal score. Displays and logs keep the bureau score.
- [x] `InterestEngine.relationshipPriceFactor()` in loan pricing; the return bonus in `expectedAnnualReturn`.
- [x] Relation changes in `LoanManager` (month-end on-time and late, payoff, default).
- [x] Terminal: an Overview line (relation and level, internal score and bonus, rate effect, return effect); a Credit tab line explaining internal vs bureau score. NexusUI: Internal Score and PBC Relation rows; `pbcRelation`, `pbcRelationLevel`, `internalScore` and `relationshipRateFactor` in the JSON.
- [x] Settings: score bonus, rate discount, return bonus, the event deltas, payoff minimum months, cap and floor levels. The main three are also in LunaLib.
- [x] Found by the tests: the foreclosure auction now restores the relation from before the foreclosure war, which already includes the goodwill lost for the default.

**Tests**
- [x] Bureau score 653: friend (+60) → internal 668, Megacorp at 3.29%/mo, bonds 4.80%/yr; neutral → 3.50%, 4.50%, 3 loans; enemy (−100) → internal 628, 4.62%, 2 loans.
- [x] 12 months paid on time → +0.022. The cap holds at 0.75. Instant take-and-repay → 0. Missed payments + default → −0.14. Two years in default → −0.49 (not hostile).

## B. Real money flows (L)
Every credit an investment earns is paid by someone in the Sector, and every loss goes somewhere. The PBC keeps a public balance sheet.

**Spikes**
- [ ] A temporary `core/EconomyDiagnostics` logs "BOS-DIAG" lines at every month end: NPC market incomes, commodity prices, the cost of a price index. **Remove it before the release.**
- [ ] Check that `MarketAPI.getNetIncome()` returns meaningful values for NPC markets. Log a sample month in-game; if it doesn't, estimate income from industries and market size.
- [ ] Measure the cost of a monthly commodity price index (`getSupplyPrice` over markets × a basket). Monthly only.

**Model**
- [ ] **Faction treasuries** (new saved class). Each major faction:
  - collects a share of its markets' net income as taxes every month;
  - pays upkeep for its military, more at war;
  - services its debt.

  A deficit is borrowed from the PBC. This replaces the random sovereign debt simulation: borrowing now comes from real deficits. Wars, lost markets and disruption push factions into debt.
- [ ] **PBC balance sheet** (new saved class).
  - Assets: reserves, loans to the player, sovereign loans, investment portfolios.
  - Liabilities: player deposits.
  - Income statement: interest earned, interest paid, fees, write-offs.
- [ ] **Where each product's return comes from:**

  | Product | Paid by | Losses come from |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Savings** | Your deposits are lent out. The rate follows the PBC's loan-book yield minus its margin, capped near the T-bill rate, and is paid from the PBC's interest income. | None for the depositor: the PBC absorbs its own losses from reserves. |
  | **Government bonds** | You lend to **a faction you choose**. Coupons come from its treasury, at a yield = base + a credit spread from its debt/income ratio, wars and stability. | Missed coupons when the treasury runs dry; a haircut on principal when the faction collapses (Nexerelin) or can't pay. |
  | **Commodity futures** | Changes in a **real in-game price index** (fuel, supplies, metals, rare metals, volatiles, organics, food, heavy machinery across markets), plus the interest earned on the collateral. | Falling prices. |
  | **Venture fund** | Stakes in **real industries** being built or recently built in NPC markets, including section C's contracts. Each stake's value follows its market: completion, growth, stability. | Disruption, raids, decivilization, a market changing hands. A few big winners and many small losses. |
  | **Military contracts** | Factions' **military spending** (treasury military upkeep; more military bases and high commands, more war), at a contractor margin. | Peace and lost military markets. |

- [ ] A PBC fee on managed products (venture, military) goes to the PBC's income statement.
- [ ] Expected returns stay near the 0.3.2 real-world levels in a calm Sector and move with the Sector's state.

**Implementation**
- [ ] `FactionTreasury` (per faction) and `PbcLedger`, both saved in `BankData` and lazily created; monthly update in `BankEconomyListener` (one market scan a month).
- [ ] `InterestEngine.calculateInvestmentReturn` now pays from the source and records the flow. Bonds gain a faction choice (`BankAccount.bondFactionId`); the terminal and branch let you pick the faction, with its yield and risk.
- [ ] A new **"Money flows"** view in the terminal (Investments tab): for each of your investments, where this month's return came from, who paid and who defaulted. The PBC's balance sheet and income statement. Each faction's treasury and debt.
- [ ] NexusUI: money flows and the PBC balance sheet in the JSON; a short summary card.
- [ ] Settings: tax share, military upkeep, PBC margin and fee, commodity basket, venture parameters.

**Tests**
- [ ] Conservation: over a simulated year, the credits paid to investors equal the credits taken from payers (treasuries, the PBC's income, price changes) plus or minus recorded losses.
- [ ] A faction at war with a falling income runs a deficit, borrows, its bond yield rises, and it defaults when it can't pay.
- [ ] Commodity futures follow the price index; savings follow the PBC's loan-book yield.
- [ ] In a calm Sector, returns stay close to 0.3.2's real-world levels.

## C. Development financing contracts (XL)
Factions finance each other's buildings. The player can receive offers, make offers, and watch NPC factions do it among themselves.

**Participants:**
- All vanilla political factions.
- The modded factions when present: **Aurora** (`uaf`), **Xhan Empire** (`xhanempire`), **Iron Shell** (`ironshell`), **ScalarTech** (`scalartech`).
- The PBC.
- The player.

Pirates, the Luddic Path and non-political factions never take part.

**Contract lifecycle**

```
OFFERED -> ACCEPTED (funds paid to the recipient) -> BUILDING
   BUILDING, built by the deadline         -> BUILT -> repaid at minimal interest  -> CLOSED (relations up)
   BUILDING, deadline missed               -> DEMAND: relations down, repay with penalty interest by a new deadline
        DEMAND paid                        -> CLOSED
        DEMAND unpaid                      -> COLLECTION (full force): PBC Collector Fleet, credits, forfeited assets
   BUILT, repayment missed                 -> COLLECTION (minimal force): smaller fleet, milder demands
```

**Design**
- [ ] **Terms.**
  - Principal ≈ the industry's build cost × (1 + a contingency margin).
  - Construction deadline = build time + slack.
  - Repayment over N months at a low rate.
  - Penalty rate and a demand deadline after a missed construction deadline.
  - The PBC acts as escrow and enforcement agent: the Clearing Hall settles inter-faction trade (LORE.md).
- [ ] **Offers to the player.** Investors are factions with a treasury surplus (section B) and a relation to the player of *Favorable* or better. They pick one of the player's colonies and a buildable industry with a good expected return, from market size, resource conditions and demand. Offers arrive as intel with Accept and Decline, and list the building, colony, amount, deadlines, rates and consequences. Accepting pays the credits to the player.
- [ ] **Player as recipient.**
  - Construction is checked daily: the industry must exist and be functional in that colony by the deadline.
  - Repayment reuses the loan machinery: a new loan type `DEVELOPMENT`, with no installments during construction and then a schedule at the low rate.
  - Collection reuses the phases and the Collection Fleet, with a "minimal force" mode: a smaller fleet and milder demands.
  - Relations change with the investor faction.
- [ ] **Player as investor.** A "Development Finance" section in the terminal and the branch office. Pick a faction (*Favorable* or better), one of its markets and an industry, then an amount. The NPC accepts or declines based on expected return, cost, its treasury, the offer's terms, the player's strength and the relationship. The NPC then really builds the industry and repays the player from its treasury. If it fails, the PBC recovers from that faction's treasury on the player's behalf, partially if the treasury is short, and relations drop.
- [ ] **NPC to NPC.** Every month, a few deals are proposed across the Sector, capped at a handful active at once, between factions at *Favorable* or better. The recipient builds the industry for real; outcomes drive relations and treasuries; they show up in an intel log and as venture-fund stakes (section B).
- [ ] **Industries:** a whitelist of vanilla industries (e.g. mining, farming, light and heavy industry, refining, fuel production, commerce, patrol HQ, orbital works), skipping anything the market can't host.
- [ ] **NPC acceptance:** utility = expected income of the building × horizon − repayment cost − the risk of the deadline, plus relationship and the investor's strength (its markets and fleets). Accept above a threshold.

**Spikes**
- [ ] Check whether an industry added to an NPC market with `startBuilding()` progresses on its own. If not, the mod keeps its own construction timer and adds the industry when the time is up: still a real building, appearing at the right time.
- [ ] Check what happens to the industry when the market changes hands mid-contract.

**Implementation**
- [ ] `DevelopmentContract` (saved) and `DevelopmentManager` (monthly offers, daily checks), plus the `DEVELOPMENT` loan type and a minimal-force collection mode.
- [ ] Intel for offers (with buttons), active contracts, and NPC deals.
- [ ] Terminal and branch screens for the player as investor.
- [ ] NexusUI: active contracts (as recipient and as investor) and recent Sector deals, in the page and the JSON.
- [ ] Settings: offer frequency, maximum active deals, margins, rates, deadlines, collection force.

**Tests (BankSim with fake markets, industries and treasuries)**
- [ ] Each lifecycle path: built and repaid; deadline missed, then demand paid; demand unpaid, then full-force collection; built but repayment missed, then minimal-force collection.
- [ ] Relations move in the right direction on each path.
- [ ] An NPC accepts a profitable deal and declines a bad one.
- [ ] NPC-to-NPC deals add the industry when it's due, and never exceed the cap.
- [ ] Modded factions take part only when they exist.
